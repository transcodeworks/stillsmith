# Third-party media

The specimen photographs in `public/rocks/` are the work of other people, used
here under Creative Commons Attribution licences. Each one is reproduced below
with the credit its licence requires: photographer, licence, and a link back to
the original.

These attributions are also rendered in the app itself, in the specimen
inspector, so the credit travels with the image rather than living only in this
file. That matters more than usual here: `rock-shelf-docs.png` and friends are
committed under `docs/src/assets/shots/`, so these photographs are redistributed
inside stillsmith's own documentation.

All images were downscaled to 600px on the long edge and re-encoded as JPEG. No
other modifications were made.

## Why gneiss, and not the slate that used to be here

The shelf used to hold Welsh roofing slate from Blaenau Ffestiniog, and no
photograph could be found for it. Every slate on Wikimedia Commons under CC BY
is a vegetated roadcut — a landscape, not a hand specimen — and the Welsh slate
that does exist is CC BY-SA, which was ruled out because share-alike would reach
the committed documentation screenshots.

Rather than carry one specimen that could never match, the fixture was changed to
a rock the photographs could support. Gneiss keeps the shelf's only
`metamorphic` entry and the cool grey the slate contributed, and the specimen is
banded and folded enough to be worth looking at in a 96px tile.

## Provenance is approximate

The photographs are of real specimens, but not of *these* specimens — this is
fixture data. Where a photograph's locality differs from the `foundAt` field,
`foundAt` is the fiction and the photograph is real:

- **Amethyst** — photographed at Planalto, Brazil; the fixture says Artigas,
  Uruguay. Same basalt formation, different side of the border.
- **Rose Quartz** — photographed at Newry, Maine; the fixture says Custer,
  South Dakota.
- **Lewisian Gneiss** — the photograph records no locality at all; St. John
  describes it only as a quartz-biotite gneiss. So the fixture's Isle of Lewis
  is invented rather than contradicted, which is the best that could be had here.

Obsidian (Glass Butte), pyrite (Navajún) and malachite (Katanga) match their
fixture localities.
